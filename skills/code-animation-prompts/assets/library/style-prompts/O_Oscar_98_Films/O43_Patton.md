## Style Prompt

1940s US war-bond lithograph poster. Flat inks: red #C2342C, navy #213261, olive #5F5C31, khaki #B7975E, on fibrous, foxed cream #E6D5B3 paper. Three values per form (bare paper, flat ink, ink plus halftone), posterised in hard steps. Brown-black key line about 1/500 of frame width, printed last; shadows as coarse 45° halftone bands (dots about 1/150 of frame width) with crayon grain; colour plates offset about 1/600 of frame width. Default: a symmetrical frontal stage, a small subject before a huge graphic shape filling most of the frame (flag, emblem, sunburst), lights as hard pale shapes or bare paper; outdoors too, light falling as a hard pale pool. Lettering optional, in the user's language. Camera: slow push-ins, one crisp pull-back. Reference: US war-bond posters, 1942–45. Avoid: airbrushed gradients, perfect registration, halftone so fine it reads grey.

### Director's notes

Clawd plays Patton: a four-star helmet, service ribbons and medals, an ivory-handled revolver, a riding crop. It walks into place beneath the stripes; cutting into the close-up, it snaps a salute, holds, lowers it, slaps the crop into its palm and nods once, as if the address is beginning. Two hard cuts, and the final pull-back shrinks it to a small dot before the giant flag.

### Storyboard from the finished film

1. Red and white stripes fill the frame (a graphic match with the last film's Warhol grid), then a pull-back as Clawd walks up from below the frame and takes its place before the giant flag. (1s)
2. Medium close-up, the camera pushing in slowly with a slight shake: Clawd under its four-star helmet snaps a salute, holds, lowers it, slaps the crop into its palm and nods firmly. (0.9s)
3. Wide: the camera pulls out past the flag to reveal the whole empty theatre — red curtain, gilded proscenium, footlights — with Clawd a small dot at centre stage. (0.5s)

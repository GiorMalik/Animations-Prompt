# O52 Kramer vs. Kramer

- Category: O Oscar 98 Films · New Hollywood 1970–1979
- What it looks like: A kitchen drawn by a child in crayon: wobbly lines, colours pressed hard into the paper grain and over the edges, labels and arrows, a smiling sun. This is how Billy sees him and his dad making French toast.
- Style: Child's crayon drawing
- Keywords: 2D
- Year: 1979

## Style Prompt

A careful 6–8-year-old's wax-crayon drawing on white paper. Crayon catches the paper grain's peaks; light pressure leaves 40–60% paper showing, heavy pressure fills it; overlaps show through. Outlines in black or dark-brown crayon, waxy, wobbling, about 1/300 of frame width. Fills are back-and-forth scribbles, strokes about 1/40 of frame width long and 1/250 wide, rows overshooting the outlines, each area in its own direction, spacing and angle uneven, never a regular mesh. Primary box: red #E0382C, orange #F28A1E, yellow #F7D23A, green #3AA345, blue #2E6BD8. Child conventions: a ground line, flattened perspective, arrowed labels, a smiling sun, one mirrored letter (in Chinese, a reversed stroke), patterns coloured cell by cell. Text in the user's language, in wobbly crayon strokes, not a typeface. Camera: shaky close-ups, then a calm drift. Avoid: neat adult rendering, random scrawl, uniform stipple or mesh, smooth gradients.

## Supplement

### Technique keywords

Crayon on textured paper, how a 6–8-year-old draws, handwritten labels

### Director's notes

Clawd plays Ted: a blue apron string tied, a spatula in hand; Billy standing on a chair cracking eggs. The first and last use of the same pan is a match cut, and the father-son relationship is told in one jump cut. Overall about 1.35×; cutting into the wide it catches the toast just in time, then gives a little jump and a look at the camera.

### Storyboard from the finished film

1. Extreme close-up of the frying pan on the first attempt: burnt toast, black crayon smoke, eggshells; handheld shake, pulling back slowly. (0.7s)
2. A match cut to the same pan at the end of the film, clean and empty: a slice of golden toast flipping out of the pan. (0.4s)
3. Under the title 'ME AND DADDY MAKE FRENCH TOAST', father and son side by side: Clawd catches the toast with the spatula, Billy breaks into a smile, a red heart pops up, and Clawd gives a proud little jump; the camera drifts up to the crayon skyline outside the window. (1.4s)

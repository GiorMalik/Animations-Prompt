# O46 The Sting

- Category: O Oscar 98 Films · New Hollywood 1970–1979
- What it looks like: Magazine illustration in the Leyendecker-to-Rockwell line: block-lit forms, parallel strokes, crisp dark outlines. The film's chapter cards imitate Saturday Evening Post covers, and the 1936 betting hall is painted the same way.
- Style: Saturday Evening Post illustration
- Keywords: 2D
- Year: 1973

## Style Prompt

1930s Saturday Evening Post cover illustration. Realist figures, correct proportions, expressive faces, built from blocky planes (lit, shadow, warm bounce from below) filled with blended strokes about 1/150 of frame width, varied in length, following the form, never stripes. Dark contour thicker on the shadow side, 1/800–1/300 of frame width. Warm key from upper left. Palette: plaster #E2C492, oak #764626, slate #28322C, brass #E2B046, ink #341E18; oak and ink darks about a third of the frame; sky plaster cooled with slate, water light slate, skin plaster plus brass. Grainy board (±3%), warm vignette. Default one-point perspective, period detail, haze, secondary figures if the story has any. Each figure is drawn once and moves whole (breathing, sway, sliding along a path). Camera: one long, smooth push in. References: J. C. Leyendecker, Norman Rockwell. Avoid: stiff cut-out figures, even outlines, muddy colour.

## Supplement

### Technique keywords

### Director's notes

Clawd plays Hooker: a light-coloured fedora, red bow tie, suspenders. The whole sequence never cuts — a single push. When the push lands, it gives the con men's signal (finger to the side of the nose), then presses its hat brim down and winks at the camera, as if pulling the audience into the con.

### Storyboard from the finished film

1. One continuous take: from a wide of the betting hall (odds board, betting windows, Gondorff in a window) pushing all the way in to Clawd as Hooker, which puts its finger to the side of its nose for the signal, then presses its hat brim down at the camera. (2.5s)

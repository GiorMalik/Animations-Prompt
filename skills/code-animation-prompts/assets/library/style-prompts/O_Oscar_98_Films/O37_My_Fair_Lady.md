# O37 My Fair Lady

- Category: O Oscar 98 Films · Swinging Sixties 1960–1969
- What it looks like: Fast, dry-brushed ink lines, with pink and magenta watercolour washes and flicks, fading into the white paper around them. The 1964 original poster was Bob Peak's ink plus pink chalk; the setting is Cecil Beaton's black-and-white Ascot.
- Style: Bob Peak fashion illustration
- Keywords: 2D
- Year: 1964

## Style Prompt

1960s fashion illustration after Bob Peak. Fast ink line, thicker in turns, dry-brush tail, a flick off; 1/600–1/250 of frame width, thinning on tight curves. Solid black for dark clothing, hats and stripes; faces bare paper #FBF7F1. Loose, soft-edged watercolour pools, darker at drying edges: pink #EE84B2 as the main field, magenta #CE3076 for hot spots, rose #F4B2CC, a touch of plum #80285C; warm grey for secondary figures and shading; directional splatter. Settings are a few lines in these washes, not literal colour. The frieze is essential: slender profile figures 9–10 heads tall repeated across the frame (the hero plus grey-wash figures or echoes), black-and-white pattern over pink; washes cover 60–80% of the frame. Camera: slow drift along the figures; graphic-mask transitions. Avoid: even-width outlines, flat digital fills, polygon-edged washes, a lone figure on an empty page.

### Director's notes

Clawd plays Eliza: a huge white hat (striped ribbon, rose, white ostrich feather), a ring of lace at the collar and hem. The opening lifts the hat brim out of the last film's black frame, and the ending pulls into the two circles of the crowd's binoculars, inside which The Sound of Music opens. Three shots, unhurried.

### Storyboard from the finished film

1. Out of the black, the brim of the Ascot hat lifts to reveal a close-up of Clawd as Eliza; it turns its gaze to the camera and drops a small curtsy as the picture gently pulls back. (0.8s)
2. Wide of Ascot: Clawd standing before two rows of stiffly posed aristocrats in Beaton black and white; a slow lateral move as it lifts its chin. (0.6s)
3. Panning along the stiffly posed crowd, everyone raising their binoculars at once, until the picture closes into the two binocular circles. (1.1s)

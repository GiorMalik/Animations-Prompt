## Style Prompt

1940s Hollywood studio black-and-white, painted like a retouched glamour still. Forms: smooth airbrushed tone blocks. Faces: short key (light on the face's far side from camera; the near cheek in shadow, holding a Rembrandt triangle of light), two catchlights per eye. Slatted shadows from anything at hand (blinds, railings, branches) fall diagonally across surfaces; light shafts show only in smoke or fog. Values: 30-40% of the frame deep black, whites small and glowing; outdoor skies mid-dark. Grade: diffusion glow, an S-curve, fine grain (±3%), soft vignette, faint flicker; fog stays local. Only the hero the story centres on keeps colour, on its body or costume (e.g. #D97757); skin and lamps stay grey. Camera: a long glide, a push to a face, a pull-back into fog. References: cinematographer Arthur Edeson (*Casablanca*, Michael Curtiz, 1942). Avoid: flat lighting, grey haze, stiff busts.

## Supplement

### Technique keywords

Airbrushed retouched portrait stills, Arthur Edeson studio lighting, soft-focus lens and silver grain

### Director's notes

Clawd plays Rick: bow tie and a champagne saucer in the café, fedora and turned-up trench collar at the airport. The café is one continuous camera move, ending on it raising its glass ('Here's looking at you, kid'); the one hard cut in the middle jumps to the airport, and a look back plus one long pull-back ends it.

### Storyboard from the finished film

1. One camera move through Rick's café: gliding past ceiling fans and smoke to Sam at the piano and Ilsa leaning on it, pushing toward her soft-focus face as tears rise, then tracking across the whole room to Clawd at a table in a bow tie, raising its champagne glass. (2.5s)
2. Hard cut to the foggy airport: a close-up of Clawd in fedora with its trench collar up, glancing back; the camera pulls back long and far, revealing Ilsa, Captain Renault, and the Electra airliner with its propellers turning in the fog. (2.5s)

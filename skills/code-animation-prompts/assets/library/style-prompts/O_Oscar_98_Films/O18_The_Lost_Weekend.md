# O18 The Lost Weekend

- Category: O Oscar 98 Films · Colour & War 1939–1949
- What it looks like: The picture is typed: monospace characters landing on a pica grid, greys built from character density and overstrikes, uneven ribbon ink. Don carries his typewriter to the pawnshop, so the whole street is typed out by the typewriter; Clawd types with an orange ribbon.
- Style: Typewriter character art
- Keywords: 2D·B&W
- Year: 1945

## Style Prompt

Type every image: one bold monospaced glyph per keystroke, fixed grid, cells 1/150 of frame width wide, twice as tall. Strike 85-95% of cells; bare paper only for highlights. Grey from a key ladder (light . : -, mid = + o, dark X # M); darks (about 30%) are overstrikes like M#. Shapes read as crisp-edged fields, clear silhouettes. Each material repeats its own keys (e.g. I H verticals, _| courses, ~ water). Signs stay legible. Uneven ribbon: half-inked strikes, a few keys always high or low. Paper #EBE6DA, ink #1D1C21; only the hero the story centres on, at least 1/3 of frame height, is struck in red-orange #D4704F, its shadow black. Hard sidelight casts long, crisp, dense shadows. The picture types itself within the first second, then holds. Reference: Paul Smith's typewriter art. Avoid: sparse frames, shapeless streaks.

### Director's notes

Clawd plays Don Birnam: a fedora typed out of characters, carrying the typewriter it wants to pawn, its four legs stepping frame by frame. The street appears around it as if typed out; then two quick hard cuts — the close-up and the CLOSED sign — finish off 'the pawnshop is shut'. The performance runs at 1.8×.

### Storyboard from the finished film

1. A one-point-perspective wide under the Third Avenue elevated railway: the street scene typed onto the paper row by row, Clawd in a fedora walking forward with its typewriter; the camera pulls back slightly. (1.3s)
2. Close-up of Clawd: the hat, eyes and the grey typewriter beside it all typed in characters, steel girders behind; the camera pushes in slowly. (0.6s)
3. Close-up of the J. GOLDMAN LOANS pawnshop sign, letters spelled out in characters, a CLOSED sign hanging below; the camera pushes in slowly. (0.6s)

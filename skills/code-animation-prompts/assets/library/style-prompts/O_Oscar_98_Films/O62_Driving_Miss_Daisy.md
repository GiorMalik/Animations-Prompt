# O62 Driving Miss Daisy

- Category: O Oscar 98 Films · Blockbuster Era 1980–1989
- What it looks like: Solid, warm Rockwell realism: clean edges, visible brushwork, afternoon sun through the oak trees, neighbours each busy with their own thing. The film is two people in a car in 1950s Atlanta — a Saturday Evening Post cover in itself.
- Style: Rockwell-style oil illustration
- Keywords: 2D
- Year: 1989

## Style Prompt

Norman Rockwell-style mid-century magazine-cover oil illustration. Near-realistic figures: head about 1/7 of body height, faces and hands modelled from 3–4 value planes, not outlines; readable expressions. Smooth modelling; faint strokes about 1/150 of frame width, following form, each within ±5% of the colour beneath. Default light: warm afternoon sun from the upper left, clear cast and dappled shadows in cool blue-grey; other light keeps a warm key and cool shadows. Palette: cream sky #E6DCC2, brick red #9A4A38, leaf greens, bottle green #1F3A24, soft skin. Gloss reflects surroundings. Background storytelling: busy bystanders where there are people, telling objects when the hero is alone. Light canvas texture, warm varnish, soft vignette. Camera: deep three-quarter wide, slow push, closer cuts ending on a hand or face. Reference: Rockwell's *Saturday Evening Post* covers (1940s–50s). Avoid: cartoon faces; texture filters; flat digital shading; empty backgrounds.

### Director's notes

Clawd plays Hoke, wearing a navy chauffeur's cap. Three hard cuts from outside the car to inside to the mirror, each closer than the last; the payoff is saved for the end: in the mirror, Miss Daisy's stern face slowly softens into a smile over the last few beats. Scene time runs about 1.8× screen time, and the car still drives unhurriedly.

### Storyboard from the finished film

1. A three-quarter front view of the dark green 1949 Hudson, driving slowly along a street lined with oaks, red-brick houses and white porches; Clawd in its chauffeur's cap at the wheel, Miss Daisy sitting stern-faced in the rear window; a slow push. (0.9s)
2. A hard cut to a locked-off camera from the back seat looking forward: Miss Daisy's big profile in the foreground, Clawd's back driving behind it, the street flowing past the windows. (0.8s)
3. A hard cut to a close-up of the rear-view mirror — the poster composition: Clawd's eyes under the cap brim on the left, Miss Daisy on the right, slowly showing a hint of a smile; a gentle push. (0.8s)

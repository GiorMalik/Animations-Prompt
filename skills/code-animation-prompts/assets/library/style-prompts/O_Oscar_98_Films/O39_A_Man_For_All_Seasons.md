# O39 A Man for All Seasons

- Category: O Oscar 98 Films · Swinging Sixties 1960–1969
- What it looks like: A classical old-master oil portrait: smooth glazing to model the form, a fur collar painted hair by hair, velvet highlights, a gold chain rendered link by link, plus aged varnish and craquelure. Holbein painted Thomas More himself in 1527 — his composition is used here.
- Style: Holbein Tudor oil portrait
- Keywords: 2D
- Year: 1966

## Style Prompt

Oil portrait after Hans Holbein the Younger. Paint in layers: a toned underlayer, directional strokes following light and shadow, soft glazes, fine detail last; smooth finish, brushwork faint in background and fabric. Render each material: fur hair by hair, velvet folds with sharp light ridges, metal with two highlights per piece, wool and leather with soft nap. Any subject becomes a formal half-length sitter in its own clothes; its setting shows through a window or in an object it holds. Palette: deep green curtain #1F4A26, black #141113, velvet red #6E0C15, gold #B0801E, fur brown #3E2818; warm, fully modelled skin. Soft light from upper left. Signature shot: the portrait against the curtain; then material close-ups, a slow pull back into a dark room lit by warm pools. Warm varnish, light craquelure. Avoid: flat fills, hard digital edges, even light, unmodelled faces.

## Supplement

### Technique keywords

Classical oil glazing, Holbein's Sir Thomas More (1527), candlelit Tudor interior

### Director's notes

Clawd plays Thomas More: a Tudor flat cap, a black fur-collared robe, red velvet sleeves, the SS gold chain, a letter sealed with red wax in hand. Coming after the spinning grassy hill, this is stillness: one cut from the extreme close-up to the portrait, then a long slow pull back; the candle is about to go out, and the darkening picture carries into the next film's night.

### Storyboard from the finished film

1. Extreme close-up of the face and the SS gold chain with its Tudor-rose pendant: Clawd as More first looks to one side, then turns its gaze to the camera and blinks; a slow push in. (0.9s)
2. The Holbein portrait composition (Tudor flat cap, fur collar, red velvet sleeves, green damask curtain, the letter in hand): a solemn nod, its gaze dropping to the letter, then a slow pull back revealing the candlelit Tudor room; the candle flame dying, the picture fading dark. (1.6s)

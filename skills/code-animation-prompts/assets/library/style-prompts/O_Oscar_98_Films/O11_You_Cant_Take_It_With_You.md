# O11 You Can't Take It with You

- Category: O Oscar 98 Films · Silent & Early Sound 1927–1938
- What it looks like: 1930s WPA silkscreen poster: six opaque flat inks, split-colour gradients, slight misregistration, printed on cotton paper. The Federal Art Project posters of 1936–39 are the American graphic language of this film's own era — and they set it apart from part 10's newspaper.
- Style: WPA silkscreen poster
- Keywords: 2D
- Year: 1938

## Style Prompt

A 1936-39 WPA (Federal Art Project) silkscreen poster. Six opaque inks printed light to dark, each later screen cut away around lighter shapes (clouds, sun): cream #F0E2C0 for light, ochre #E0A842 and vermilion #C84A2A for sky and warm planes, teal #22686E for water and shade, the hero's own ink (e.g. #D97757, only on the hero the story centres on), brown keyline #322018 last. Shadows: a darker ink overprinted, never grey. Big areas get split-fountain blends: two inks in 2-3 coarse steps, the middle one their overprint. Screens 1-3 px off register; wandering stencil edges, squeegee streaks, warm rag-paper grain (±5%) on every flat. Figures fill 1/2-2/3 of frame height: big planes, crescent shadows, expressive faces. A bold capital title band, user's language. Slow pushes; screens pop in one by one. Avoid: halftone dots, smooth gradients, clean vector flats.

### Director's notes

Clawd plays Grandpa Vanderhof: round metal-framed glasses, a harmonica, a cane leaning against the chair. Everyone in the house is busy with their own thing, and the fireworks in the basement get bigger and bigger in the last shot. Two hard cuts: living room → harmonica → fireworks.

### Storyboard from the finished film

1. The whole Vanderhof living room, like a poster: Clawd as Grandpa, round glasses, sitting in a cyan armchair playing the harmonica; Essie dancing on tiptoe by the window; Kolenkhov calling out the beat; De Pinna poking his head out of the basement trapdoor; a gentle pull. (0.8s)
2. Close-up of Clawd: round glasses, harmonica, notes floating up, De Pinna at the edge of the frame; push in. (0.6s)
3. Back to the living room as the basement fireworks go off: rockets, starbursts and columns of smoke filling the house; the camera pulls back. (1.1s)

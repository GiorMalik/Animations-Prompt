# O76 The Lord of the Rings: The Return of the King

- Category: O Oscar 98 Films · New Millennium 2000–2009
- What it looks like: Soft watercolour-and-pencil landscapes with deep aerial perspective, plus an ink-line map on parchment. The film's concept art was painted by Alan Lee and John Howe, so the journey is told as the trilogy was first drawn.
- Style: Alan Lee-style watercolour + Tolkien map
- Keywords: 2D
- Year: 2003

## Style Prompt

Fantasy concept-art watercolour and pencil on warm paper. Transparent washes, granulated (pigment speckle, ±6% brightness), a few blooms, darker pooled edges; every contour and ridge carries a dark-brown pencil line (about 1/800 of frame width), short hatching on shadow sides. Aerial perspective: 3–5 receding planes, each paler and cooler. Light is the subject: one strong source per scene (sun, fire, lamp, molten glow) burning through haze, its ember glow on about a quarter of the frame, about half the frame in deep umber and violet shadow. Palette: parchment #E9DCC0, deep umber #3A2418, ember orange #E07A2E, hot red #B23A1E only at the light's core, lavender #B8A8C8 for distance. Journeys may cut to a Tolkien-style map, never the main look. Camera: long lateral pans, slow push-ins to the hero. References: Alan Lee, John Howe. Avoid: smooth digital gradients; vector shapes; pale frames.

## Supplement

### Technique keywords

Watercolour-and-pencil concept illustration, Tolkien-style parchment map, Alan Lee and John Howe

### Director's notes

Clawd plays Frodo: a grey-green cloak collar, a Lothlórien leaf brooch, the Ring on a chain. As a key film of the decade it gets 12 beats: the map lays out the whole road, the beacons carry the camera across the mountains, the Eye is one brief threat, and the sequence closes on the Ring raised high. Hard cuts between shots, long movements inside each shot.

### Storyboard from the finished film

1. Looking down at the parchment map: the camera pushes in from the whole map to the Shire, then flies along the red dashed line toward Mordor, Clawd as small as a chess piece walking the route. (1.3s)
2. Wide: snow mountains at dusk floating on a sea of cloud, the nearest beacon lit first, the camera slowly panning right and pulling back slightly as the beacons on the ridges light up one after another. (1.9s)
3. Close-up: the Eye of Sauron atop Barad-dûr, the camera pushing in as it turns and sweeps out a beam of light. No Clawd. (0.4s)
4. At the edge of the crack of Mount Doom: the camera pushes from a wide of the basalt tongue above the lava to Clawd, the volcano and the Eye behind it, as it lifts the glowing Ring from the edge of the abyss and raises it high. (1.5s)

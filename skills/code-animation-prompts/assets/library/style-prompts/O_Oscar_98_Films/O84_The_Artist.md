## Style Prompt

A modern homage to late-1920s silent film in black and white. Clean tones: glowing highlights, soft mid-greys, deep blacks; a gentle S-curve, grey halation around lights, slight flicker (±3%), fine grain, rare dust, a 1.33 pillarbox inside 16:9. Intertitles on black with an Art Deco border and spaced capitals, in the user's language. Settings: the subject's world staged as grand, ordered interiors or stage sets (e.g. a wrought-iron staircase, a gilt proscenium, a crowd in backlit silhouette); figures in elegant profile. The one hero subject the story centres on is the only coloured thing, wholly in tones of terracotta #D97757; lamps and fire stay grey. Camera: smooth push-ins and pull-backs, title cards flashed in, cuts landing on gestures (a bow, a wave). Reference: cinematographer Guillaume Schiffman (*The Artist*, 2011). Avoid: heavy scratches; sepia tint; colour bleeding into the greys.

### Director's notes

Clawd plays George Valentin: a white wing collar, black bow tie, a silk top hat and cane, plus the Jack Russell Uggie. Five fast cuts, near real speed: tipping the hat on the stairs, the BANG! title card flashing, the little dog falling, and a bow as the audience rises. Clawd is the only colour.

### Storyboard from the finished film

1. A wide of the staircase pushing in: Clawd as George standing on the landing, tipping its top hat to Peppy coming up the stairs, extras crossing up and down, Uggie beside it. (1s)
2. Close-up: the raised hand taking off the hat, Uggie looking up at it; a slight push. (0.5s)
3. A full-screen title card: BANG! written inside an Art Deco border. (0.3s)
4. On the stage, Clawd pointing its fingers like a gun, Uggie falling back and playing dead; a slow push. (0.5s)
5. Wide of the premiere: THE END on the screen, Constance applauding at the right, the audience silhouettes rising, Clawd tipping its hat and bowing with its cane, Uggie at its feet; the camera pulls back slightly, the grand curtain starting to close. (0.9s)
